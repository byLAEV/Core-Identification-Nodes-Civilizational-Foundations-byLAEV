# Core Identification Nodes — Civilizational Foundations byLAEV

## Description

Core Identification Nodes byLAEV is a civilizational research and development initiative dedicated to exploring, defining, and establishing the philosophical, theoretical, conceptual, methodological, and technological foundations of a decentralized, interoperable, and interconnected ecosystem of individual nodes.

The project investigates how human events, histories, peer-to-peer communication, and distributed ledgers can participate in the development of a shared civilizational infrastructure.

Its foundational scope includes the study of individual nodes, the recognition and registration of human events, the continuity of historical records, and the possibility of establishing internationally interoperable systems of distributed cooperation.

Core Identification Nodes byLAEV approaches these questions as an integrated civilizational challenge rather than exclusively as a technological or administrative problem.

---

## Core Equation

**H → P2P → L**

- **H — Histories**
- **P2P — Peer-to-Peer Communication**
- **L — Ledgers**

The equation expresses the communication of histories through peer-to-peer relationships and networks, with Ledgers serving as a means of recording and organizing those histories.

Histories are the information being communicated; P2P provides the communication relationship and mechanism; Ledgers provide recording and organization structures. The equation is the conceptual foundation for examining how histories can be communicated, connected, recorded, and consulted across multiple nodes and Ledgers.

# Foundational Writing and Validation Standard

Every assertion incorporated into Core Identification Nodes byLAEV must meet explicit criteria of definition, foundation, coherence, scope, and verification.

Assertions must be distinguished according to their epistemological nature: axioms, definitions, premises, deductions, empirical hypotheses, normative principles, and protocol rules.

No conclusion may be presented without its premises. No hypothesis may be presented as an established fact. No technological proposal may be confused with a demonstrated universal necessity.

Every objection must be examined in relation to the definitions, premises, logical relationships, and validity conditions of the proposition being questioned.

The knowledge developed by the project must remain open to verification, justified correction, and evolution while preserving the coherence of its foundational principles.
