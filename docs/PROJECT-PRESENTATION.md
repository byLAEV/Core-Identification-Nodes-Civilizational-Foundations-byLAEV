## About the Proposal

I present **Core Identification Nodes — Civilizational Foundations byLAEV** as the result of years of research and conceptual development concerning decentralized ecosystems and their potential applications in the organization of human societies.

The proposal explores the design of a worldwide decentralized, interoperable, and interconnected infrastructure of individual nodes. Its purpose is to investigate how human events can be communicated, recognized, recorded, and connected through distributed cooperation.

One of the fundamental problems addressed by this research is the fragmentation of birth-registration systems and the absence of a globally agreed framework for establishing an interoperable sequence of birth events.

My research seeks to develop an architectural framework through which new forms of cooperation, communication, and record organization can be explored using peer-to-peer networks and distributed Ledgers.

Its conceptual foundation is expressed through the following equation:

**H → P2P → L**

- **H — Histories:** the histories containing human events and information.
- **P2P — Peer-to-Peer Communication:** communication and relationships among independent peers and nodes.
- **L — Ledgers:** distributed structures through which histories can be recorded and organized.

The equation expresses the communication of histories through peer-to-peer relationships and networks, with Ledgers serving as structures for recording and organizing those histories.

## The Connection with My Studies in Architecture

An important aspect of this process is that, although this line of research emerged from my interests and professional work in technology, decentralized systems, and blockchain, I gradually recognized its direct relationship with my university studies in Architecture.

Through the natural evolution of my work and the circumstances of my academic journey, I began to recognize that many of the questions I was investigating could also be approached through architectural thinking: the organization of complex systems, the design of infrastructure, the relationships between components, the planning of functional structures, and the development of models capable of responding to human needs.

This connection has opened a new academic and professional dimension for my research.

I now intend to develop and formally present this proposal in connection with the subject and research direction I plan to pursue for the defense of my Bachelor's Degree in Architecture.

My intention is not simply to incorporate blockchain technology into an architectural project. Rather, I want to explore how architectural thinking and design principles can contribute to the development of a decentralized sociotechnical infrastructure with civilizational scope.

## Academic and Professional Purpose

My purpose is to consolidate this research into a formal academic proposal, establish its foundations, develop its methodology, and define a conceptual and functional architecture whose possibilities for implementation can be examined.

I see this project as a convergence of my research experience in decentralized technology, my professional work as a solution designer, and my university education in Architecture.

**Core Identification Nodes is therefore a proposal that has emerged from years of research and has found in Architecture a field for further development, formalization, and academic exploration.**

The project is being developed progressively as research, conceptual design, and systems architecture. Its proposed mechanisms and potential applications remain subjects for formal development, analysis, and validation.
