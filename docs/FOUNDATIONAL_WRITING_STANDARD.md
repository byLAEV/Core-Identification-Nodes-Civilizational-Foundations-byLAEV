# FOUNDATIONAL_WRITING_STANDARD.md

## Core Identification Nodes byLAEV

**Document Classification:** Foundational Governance  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Draft

### 1. Purpose

This document establishes the intellectual, epistemological, methodological, and editorial standards governing the development of Core Identification Nodes byLAEV.

Its purpose is to ensure that every document contributes to a coherent body of knowledge supported by explicit definitions, transparent reasoning, consistent principles, and verifiable claims.

### 2. Fundamental Writing Principles

All project documentation must comply with the following principles:

1. Conceptual precision.
2. Logical consistency.
3. Explicit premises.
4. Traceable reasoning.
5. Terminological consistency.
6. Evidence-based assertions.
7. Methodological reproducibility.
8. Intellectual transparency.
9. Documented evolution.
10. Distinction between established knowledge and proposed models.

### 3. Epistemological Classification

Every fundamental proposition must be classified according to its nature:

- Axioms and foundational principles.
- Formal definitions.
- Explicit premises.
- Logical deductions.
- Empirical claims.
- Testable hypotheses.
- Normative principles.
- Architectural propositions.
- Protocol specifications.

Different categories must be evaluated according to appropriate standards of validity.

### 4. Argumentation Requirements

Every substantial argument must establish:

- The proposition being presented.
- Its conceptual definitions.
- Its underlying premises.
- Its logical development.
- Its supporting evidence, where applicable.
- Its scope and limitations.
- Its conditions of validity.
- Its implications for the project.

### 5. Verification and Critical Examination

No proposition shall be considered established solely because it appears plausible, authoritative, or widely accepted.

Claims must be open to examination through appropriate logical, empirical, mathematical, or methodological procedures.

Criticism must address the actual proposition, its premises, its definitions, or its evidence.

Neither arbitrary rejection nor unconditional acceptance constitutes validation.

### 6. Historical and Documentary Integrity

Changes to approved foundational documents must be traceable.

Previous versions, amendments, supporting arguments, and approval states must remain documented according to the project's version-control practices.

### 7. Document Approval Lifecycle

Every document follows three principal stages:

- **DRAFT** — Under development.
- **REVIEW** — Under examination and validation.
- **APPROVED** — Accepted as an official project reference.

Approval does not make a document immune to evidence-based revision.

### 8. Language Standard

English is the official language of Core Identification Nodes byLAEV documentation.

Technical terminology must remain consistent throughout the repository.

### 9. Governing Principle

The intellectual integrity of Core Identification Nodes byLAEV depends on the strength of its foundations, the transparency of its reasoning, and its capacity to withstand independent examination.

**Foundational documentation must be rigorous without becoming dogmatic, universal in ambition without assuming universal agreement, and open to evolution without losing conceptual coherence.**
